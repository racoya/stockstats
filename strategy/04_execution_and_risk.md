# Execution & Risk Management: Protecting the Capital

## 1. Objective
To define the mechanisms that translate mathematical signals into live market orders while relentlessly protecting the proprietary trading capital from slippage, flash crashes, and algorithmic runaways.

## 2. Order Routing & Execution (The Hands)
The system must never use simple "Market Orders" (which guarantee execution but ruin the entry price).

*   **Smart Order Routing (SOR):** For crypto, the SOR scans the order books of Binance, Kraken, and Coinbase simultaneously and fractions the limit order across exchanges to absorb liquidity without moving the price.
*   **Execution Algorithms (TWAP/VWAP):** Large orders are never dumped at once. The system strictly utilizes Time-Weighted Average Price (TWAP) or Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) algorithms to split a $100k order into 100 x $1k micro-orders over a specific timeframe to disguise our footprint and minimize slippage.
*   **Passive vs. Aggressive Execution:** The Logic engine dictates urgency. 
    *   *High Urgency (Stop Loss):* Aggressive crossing of the spread.
    *   *Low Urgency (Mean Reversion Entry):* Passive posting of limit orders at the bid (Maker fees) to capture the spread.

## 3. The "Kill Switches" (Systemic Risk Management)
Every algorithmic system has bugs. The execution engine must include hard-coded circuits that immediately halt operations, bypassing the Logic Engine entirely.

*   **Fat Finger / Max Order Size:** A hard limit on the max nominal value of any single order generated by the system (e.g., no order $>$ $50k). If the math formula bugs out and outputs $50M, the execution engine rejects it at the gate.
*   **Max Drawdown Circuit Breaker:** If the aggregate portfolio (or a specific strategy pod) drops by $X\%$ within a 24-hour window, all open positions are immediately liquidated to cash, and the API keys are automatically disabled until a human Admin physically unlocks them.
*   **Latency Spiked & Disconnects:** If the "ping" to the exchange API exceeds $X$ milliseconds, or if WebSocket heartbeat packets are missed, the system immediately cancels all open limit orders. You cannot trade a $3X$ Sigma band if your data is 5 seconds old.

## 4. Slippage & Friction Auditing
The execution engine must constantly audit its own performance against the theoretical models.

*   **Expected vs. Actual Fill Price:** Every execution logs the *Expected Price* (what the math engine authorized) vs. the *Actual Fill Price* (slipped entry). If the average slippage delta exceeds the model's tolerance threshold, the strategy is automatically paused for human review.
